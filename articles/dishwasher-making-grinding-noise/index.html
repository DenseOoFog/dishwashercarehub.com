Noises and operation

Dishwasher Grinding Noise: Match the Sound to the Cycle

Do not diagnose a pump from the word “grinding” alone. First identify when the sound occurs, whether water enters and drains, and whether the sound follows the spray-arm rhythm.

Published April 18, 2026 · Updated August 9, 2026 · By Dishwasher Care Lab editorial team

Use the dishwasher noise decoder

First observation

Note the cycle stage

Fill, wash, drain, and post-cycle drying produce different normal and abnormal sounds.

Fast answer

When to stop immediately

Stop the cycle for smoke, scorching, a burning or hot-plastic smell, repeated breaker trips, or active leaking. Disconnect power if it is safe and arrange qualified help.

Quick distinction

Some rough sounds are normal—and some combinations are not

A dishwasher changes valves, pumps water through spray arms, drains several times, opens the detergent dispenser, and may run a drying fan. Whirlpool describes swishing during wash, a soft drain-pump hum, a brief gurgle near the sink, and some clicks as normal stage-related sounds. GE Appliances likewise lists swishing, humming, control clicks, drain changes, and a dispenser snap among sounds that can be normal.

The useful clue is what happens at the same time. A rhythmic knock during spray action often points to a spray arm touching a utensil or tall dish. A harsh grind while the machine fails to fill deserves a different response. A persistent hum or grind during drain combined with water left in the tub points toward the drain path, but it still does not identify a specific failed component.

Ten-minute triage

Four safe checks before another full cycle

  1. Record the stage. Write down whether the sound begins during fill, circulation, drain, or after the cycle. Note any error code before resetting anything.
  2. Disconnect power. Wait until all motion stops. Never bypass the door latch to listen with the door open, and never reach into the tub while the appliance is energized.
  3. Inspect the load and spray-arm clearance. Move tall utensils, pan handles, cutting boards, and lightweight items. Gently turn each user-accessible spray arm to make sure it does not strike the load.
  4. Inspect only the routine-access filter area. Follow the model manual. Wear cut-resistant gloves if broken glass is possible, use a flashlight instead of feeling blindly, and do not remove a pump cover unless the manual explicitly treats it as user maintenance.

If the sound clearly came from the load and the obstruction is removed, monitor one normal cycle from a safe distance. If the sound is sharp, continuous, worsening, or paired with poor filling or drainage, do not keep running comparison cycles.

During wash

Grinding, rattling, or knocking while the spray arms run

GE Appliances says rhythmic knocking is most likely caused by a spray arm hitting something inside the machine. Bosch also lists incorrect loading and spray-arm contact among common causes of knocking or rattling. This is why timing matters: contact usually repeats at a steady rhythm as the arm rotates, while loose dishes may chatter more irregularly as water pressure changes.

Repositioning the load is a legitimate first check. It is not a reason to force a bent arm, enlarge a spray opening, or dismantle the circulation system. If the arm has visible cracking, will not sit correctly after being installed according to the manual, or the same harsh sound remains with clear movement, arrange service or model-specific support.

For a careful cleaning workflow, use the dishwasher spray-arm cleaning guide.

During fill

Grinding or buzzing when the tub is not getting water

GE’s abnormal-noise guidance associates grinding with the circulation pump operating when there is no water in the machine. The household-safe conclusion is not “replace the pump.” It is “stop repeated dry operation and investigate why water is absent.”

Confirm the dishwasher supply valve has not been closed and that the visible inlet hose is not sharply kinked. Check whether the display reports a no-fill or water-supply code, then use the exact model manual. The inlet valve, float switches, wiring, and concealed plumbing are not appropriate live-DIY checks. Continue with the dishwasher not filling guide or arrange qualified service.

During drain

A short hum can be normal; retained water changes the diagnosis

Manufacturers describe drain-pump humming and brief gurgling as normal on many models. Bosch notes that grinding or buzzing may be heard as water is forced down the drain. Therefore, a drain-stage sound without another symptom is weak evidence of failure.

If water remains after the cycle, the observation becomes more useful. Start with the removable filter, visible air gap, recently changed disposal connection, and visible drain-hose route. Do not reach into a hidden impeller or remove the machine’s lower service panel. Use the standing-water diagnosis tool to separate an internal drain problem from sink-side backflow.

Avoid damage

Do not use these shortcuts

  • Do not run repeated noisy cycles hoping a hard object will clear itself.
  • Do not pour drain cleaner into the dishwasher or use improvised solvents around seals and pumps.
  • Do not reach blindly below the filter; broken glass and sharp food fragments can be difficult to see.
  • Do not add water or lubricant to quiet a pump unless the exact manufacturer instructions call for it.
  • Do not assume that “grinding” proves a motor, bearing, chopper, or pump failure. Those conclusions require model-specific inspection and testing.

Service threshold

When the noise belongs with a technician or plumber

  • Arrange appliance service when a harsh sound remains after clearing the load and completing only manual-approved filter care.
  • Arrange service when the dishwasher repeatedly fails to fill, circulate, or drain, or displays a recurring error code.
  • Use a qualified plumber when the sound is clearly in household piping or the sink-side drain backs up.
  • Stop immediately for smoke, scorching, burning odor, repeated breaker trips, or leaking near electrical components.

A short recording made from a safe distance can help service personnel, especially when you also note the model number, selected cycle, exact stage, and whether water entered or remained.

Common questions

Dishwasher grinding-noise FAQ

Is a dishwasher supposed to make a grinding sound?

Some model owners describe normal pump or control sounds as grinding. A brief, repeatable sound at a cycle transition with normal filling, washing, and drainage may be normal. A new harsh sound with a performance problem deserves investigation.

Why does my dishwasher grind only during washing?

Check whether the sound repeats with spray-arm rotation. A utensil, pan handle, tall plate, or loose item can contact the arm or chatter under water pressure.

Why does it grind while draining?

A drain pump can make normal operating noise. If the tub also retains water, inspect only the user-accessible filter and visible drain route before arranging service.

Can I keep using it if dishes still come out clean?

Compare a brief stage-specific sound with the model manual. Stop if it is new and worsening, continuous, or joined by heat, electrical, leak, fill, or drainage symptoms.
